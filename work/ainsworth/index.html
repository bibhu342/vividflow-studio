How it works

Four steps. Nothing happens on the first visit.

  1. 01

    Consultation

    Thirty minutes with Sarah. Medical history, what you'd like to change, and an honest view on whether treatment will get you there.

  2. 02

    Written plan

    Options, expected results, risks and the full cost, in writing. Then at least 48 hours to think it over.

  3. 03

    Treatment

    Performed by Sarah, in a clinical room, with aftercare written down before you leave.

  4. 04

    Two-week review

    A follow-up is included in every plan. If something isn't as agreed, this is where it's put right.

Why there are no before-and-after photos here.

Results depend on your anatomy, the product used and your aftercare — a photo of someone else's face tells you very little about yours. Where patients have given written consent, Sarah will show relevant cases at your consultation, alongside what could go wrong.

Questions people ask before booking

The honest answers.

Who actually performs the treatment?

Sarah does — every time. She is an NMC registered nurse prescriber, and she assesses, plans and treats. Treatment is never handed to an unregistered practitioner.

Does it hurt?

Most people describe a pinch or a brief sting. Topical anaesthetic is used where it helps, and many fillers contain a local anaesthetic. Sarah will tell you what to expect for your specific treatment at consultation.

How much downtime should I plan for?

For most skin and injectable treatments, mild redness, swelling or bruising for a few days. Peels and microneedling can mean a week of visible recovery. Your written plan states the expected downtime before you agree to anything.

What if I don't like the result?

That is what the two-week review is for. Hyaluronic acid filler can be adjusted or dissolved. Sarah plans conservatively — it is far easier to add at review than to take away.

Is the consultation really free?

Yes. It is thirty minutes, there is no charge and no obligation, and you won't be treated on the same day.

Can anyone be treated?

No. Treatment is only for over-18s, and is not offered during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or with certain medical conditions and medications. That is part of what the consultation checks.
